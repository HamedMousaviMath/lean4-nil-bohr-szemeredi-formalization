2 Finite counting and the selector

Definition 1 Configuration and arithmetic rescaling
#

Write \([N]=\{ 1,\ldots ,N\} \) and \(P_0=0\). The original integer polynomials satisfy \(P_i(0)=0\) and \(1\le \deg P_1{\lt}\cdots {\lt}\deg P_\ell =d\). A configuration is an \(x\in \mathbb Z\) and positive integer \(n\) such that every \(x+P_i(n)\) belongs to \(A\), and the map from \(i\in \{ 0,\ldots ,\ell \} \) to these points is injective. For a positive integer \(q\) put \(P_{i,q}(y)=P_i(qy)/q\). For \(P(y)=a_ey^e+\cdots +a_1y\), \(e\ge 1\), the \((C,q)\)-coefficient condition is

\[ |a_j|\le C|a_e|\quad (1\le j{\lt}e),\qquad a_e=a'_e q^{e-1},\qquad a'_e\in \mathbb Z,\quad 0{\lt}|a'_e|\le C. \]

For \(R_0=0\) and a given family \(R_1,\ldots ,R_{k-1}\), define

\[ T^{\mathbf R}_{N,M,v}(\mathbf f) =\frac{1}{NM}\sum _{x\in \mathbb Z}\sum _{y\in [M]} v(y)\prod _{i=0}^{k-1} f_i(x+R_i(y)). \]

Manuscript label. d:Cq

The normalization uses the spatial scale \(N\) and the parameter length \(M\). The support of \(f_0\) bounds the spatial sum because \(R_0=0\). From \(P_i(0)=0\), the coefficient of \(y^j\) in \(P_{i,q}\) is \(q^{j-1}\) times that in \(P_i\). This gives the coefficient condition with a fixed \(C\) for every \(q\ge 1\). The same arithmetic gives the successive rescalings. If \(q'=q^{B+1}\) then

\[ q^B P_{i,q'}(y)=P_{i,q}(q^B y),\qquad q'y+1=q(q^B y)+1. \]

An affine embedding \(x\mapsto a+q^B x\) therefore preserves the parameter prime, its selector value, and pairwise distinctness.

Definition 2 Phase tests and Gowers normalization
#

For a finite nonempty progression \(P\) and real polynomial tests, put

\[ \lVert v\rVert _{u^s[P]} =\sup _{\substack {\phi \in \mathbb R[y]\\ \deg \phi {\lt}s}} \lvert \frac1{|P|}\sum _{y\in P}v(y)\mathrm e(-\phi (y))\rvert , \qquad \mathrm e(t)=e^{2\pi it}. \]

For a finitely supported \(g:\mathbb Z\to \mathbb C\), let

\[ \lVert g\rVert ^{2^s}_{U^s(\mathbb Z)} =\sum _{x,h_1,\ldots ,h_s\in \mathbb Z} \prod _{\omega \in \{ 0,1\} ^s} \mathcal C^{|\omega |}g(x+\omega \cdot h), \qquad \lVert g\rVert _{U^s[I]} =\frac{\lVert g\mathbf1_I\rVert _{U^s(\mathbb Z)}}{\lVert \mathbf1_I\rVert _{U^s(\mathbb Z)}}. \]

Here \(\mathcal C\) is complex conjugation and \(I\) is a finite nonempty interval or progression. For \(s=1\) this is the absolute average. Normalized monotonicity bounds \(U^r[I]\) by \(U^s[I]\) up to constants depending only on \(r,s\), for \(1\le r\le s\). Multiplication by \(\mathrm e(\phi )\) with \(\deg \phi {\lt}s\) preserves \(U^s[I]\), so these facts also give \(u^s[I]\ll _s U^s[I]\).

Proof of the finite norm estimates.

For each cube coordinate \(i\), reflect the array of vertex functions onto the two \(i\)-faces. The exact substitution \(y=x+h_i\) factors the sum into a correlation of two functions of the other directions. The zero extension allows both base sums to run over the same interval. Cauchy–Schwarz bounds the squared mixed form by the two reflected forms. Repeating in all coordinates bounds the mixed form by the product of the vertex norms. Expanding the vertices for \(f+g\) gives the triangle inequality. The real cube power \(Q_s(f)\) is also a sum of squared magnitudes of the \((s-1)\)-direction correlations. Viewing \(Q_s(f)\) as the sum of the \(s\)-direction correlations and using Cauchy–Schwarz gives \(Q_s(f)^2\le (2W+1)^sQ_{s+1}(f)\) for an interval of width \(W\). The indicator cube count lies between \((W/(s+1))^{s+1}\) and \(W(2W)^s\). For the lower bound take the base point and \(s\) nonnegative directions in intervals of length \(\lfloor (W-1)/(s+1)\rfloor +1\). The base and the singleton vertices bound the upper count. Division by the indicator powers therefore gives a uniform adjacent-order bound.

An affine bijection from \([M]\) to a positive-step progression matches every surviving cube because each direction is divisible by the step. The same bijection transports the indicator and takes ordinary polynomials of degree below \(s\) to polynomials of degree below \(s\). The norm at order one is the magnitude of the mean. Phase invariance and order comparison then bound each phase test before the supremum. For dilation on \([b,L+b]\), use the \(q\) linear characters of \(\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z\) to restrict to the residue class \(b\). Their coefficients have total absolute mass one. Remove the ambient point \(b\) using the triangle inequality and bound its singleton norm using the all-zero directions. Exact affine cube transport and the volume bounds compare the ambient width \(L+1\) with \(\lfloor L/q\rfloor \). Their ratio is at most \(2q\), which gives the power \(q^{(s+1)/2^s}\). All steps are checked in Lemma 23.

Circle Fourier summability.

For a \(K\)-Lipschitz cutoff \(\chi \) with coefficients \(c_h\), the difference \(\chi (\cdot +t)-\chi \) has \(L^2\) norm at most \(K|t|\) in unit Haar measure. Its \(h\)-th coefficient is \((\mathrm e(ht)-1)c_h\). Parseval bounds the sum of squared moduli on each finite frequency set. Divide by \(t^2\) for \(t\ne 0\) and let \(t\to 0\) on that finite set. This gives a uniform bound for the partial sums of \(\sum _h (2\pi h)^2|c_h|^2\). Cauchy–Schwarz against the summable squares \(h^{-2}\) bounds \(\sum _{h\ne 0}|c_h|\). The zero coefficient is at most \(\lVert \chi \rVert _\infty \). Mathlib’s Fourier reconstruction then identifies the absolutely convergent series with \(\chi \).

Definition 3 Heisenberg and bracket selectors
#

Put

\[ \theta =\frac{\alpha \beta }{2},\qquad Z(n)=\theta n(n-1)-\alpha n\lfloor \beta n\rfloor , \qquad Z_{\rm br}(n)=\alpha (n+1)\lfloor \beta (n+1)\rfloor . \]

The assumptions are \(c_\alpha =\inf _{m\ge 1}m\lVert m\alpha \rVert _{\mathbb T}{\gt}0\) and \(\beta \in (0,1)\) with an increasing sequence of reduced rationals \(a_j/r_j\) of increasing prime denominator \(r_j\), tending to \(\beta \), such that

\[ r_{j+1}\asymp \exp (\exp (\sqrt{\log r_j})),\qquad 0{\lt}\beta -a_j/r_j\ll r_{j+1}^{-1}. \]

Write \(\mathbb T=\mathbb R/\mathbb Z\) and let \(J\) be an open arc of length in \((0,1)\). The return set is \(\mathcal B_{Z,J}=\{ n\in \mathbb Z_{{\gt}0}:Z(n)\bmod 1\in J\} \). For \(V=Z\) or \(Z_{\rm br}\) set

\[ \mathcal P_{V,J}=\{ p\in \mathbb P:V(p-1)\bmod 1\in J\} ,\qquad \pi _{V,J}(N)=|\mathcal P_{V,J}\cap [N]|. \]

Set \(\Lambda _q(y)=(\varphi (q)/q)\Lambda (qy+1)\), \(F_q(y)=F(Z(qy))\) and \(\mu _F=\int _\mathbb TF\).

Let \(H=\mathbb R^3\) have multiplication \((x,y,z)(x',y',z')=(x+x',y+y',z+z'+xy')\). With \(\Gamma =\mathbb Z^3\) and \(g_0=(\alpha ,\beta ,0)\), direct multiplication gives \(g_0^n=(n\alpha ,n\beta ,\theta n(n-1))\) for all integer \(n\). Right multiplication by \((u,v,w)\in \Gamma \) changes \(z-x\lfloor y\rfloor \) by \(w-u(\lfloor y\rfloor +v)\in \mathbb Z\). Thus on the chart \(0{\lt}\{ y\} {\lt}1\), the vertical phase \(z-x\lfloor y\rfloor \bmod 1\) gives \(Z(n)\bmod 1\) on the orbit. The Haar probability measure projects to uniform measure on the horizontal torus, and vertical fibre measure is uniform. For \(n\ge 1\), irrationality of \(\beta \) ensures \(\{ \beta n\} {\gt}0\), so these are returns to the open vertical window of measure \(|J|\).

Admissible \(\beta \) are dense in \((0,1)\). To see this, start with a reduced rational in the middle third of a prescribed interval, with large prime denominator \(r_1\). Bertrand’s postulate supplies a prime \(r_{j+1}\) comparable to the prescribed growth function. Choose \(a_{j+1}=\lfloor r_{j+1}a_j/r_j\rfloor +1\). The successive positive increments are less than \(1/r_{j+1}\). The denominator growth bounds the entire tail by a constant times its first term. A large \(r_1\) keeps the fractions within the prescribed interval. This also gives \(\beta -a_j/r_j=o(r_j^{-D})\) for every fixed \(D\). The positive errors and rational spacing exclude rational limits, giving the Liouville property. Lean checks this density construction and the implication from admissibility to the Liouville property.

Proposition 15 gives the introductory counterexample using faster prime-denominator approximations.

Proof in the manuscript ▼

The first step removes the weight while retaining a polynomial fraction of spatial differences. By Cauchy–Schwarz,

\[ \delta ^2N^2\le \mathbb E_{y\in [M]} \lvert \sum _x\prod _{i=0}^{k-1} f_i(x+P_i(y))\rvert ^2 =\sum _h\mathbb E_{y\in [M]}\sum _x\prod _{i=0}^{k-1} \Delta _h f_i(x+P_i(y)), \]

where \(\Delta _h f(x)=f(x+h)\overline{f(x)}\). Only \(O_C(N)\) terms in \(h\) are nonzero and each is \(O_C(N)\). The lower bound therefore gives \(\gg _C\delta ^2N\) shifts \(h\) with unnormalized count of size \(\gg _C\delta ^2N\). Translate the spatial supports into \([N']\) with \(N'\asymp _C N\) and \(N'\ge N\). The KMTT inverse theorem applies at length \(M\), and its normalized threshold is a fixed multiple of \(\delta ^2\). Its small alternative \(M\ll (q/\delta )^{O(1)}\) gives \(N{\lt}q^{d-1}(M+1)^d\ll (q/\delta )^{O(1)}\). As \(q\le \exp ((\log N)^{1/10})\), any fixed power of \(q\) is at most \(N^{1/2}\) for sufficiently large \(N\), giving the required small alternative.

In the other branch, KMTT supplies an auxiliary \(q'_h\ll \delta ^{-O(1)}\), an exponent \(b_h=O_d(1)\) and \(1\)-bounded \(\varphi _{h,i}\) such that the correlation with \(\Delta _hf_i\) is at least \(\delta ^{O(1)}N\) up to a fixed constant, and

\[ |\varphi _{h,i}(x+q'_h q^{b_h} t)-\varphi _{h,i}(x)| \ll (q/\delta )^{O(1)}M^{-\deg P_i}|t|. \]

Take \(B\) to be a uniform bound for \(b_h\) and choose \(D_0\ge B\) large. Let \(V_i=\lfloor M^{e_i}(q/\delta )^{-D_0}\rfloor \), \(e_i=\deg P_i\). A value inside the floor below \(2\) forces the small alternative by the same bound on powers of \(q\). Since \(L(q/\delta )^{-D_0}\le 1\), the inequality \(LV_i\le N\) follows. The family of full blocks meeting the support contributes \(O_C(N/V_i)\) progressions \(P=a+L[V_i]\), including cells at the ends.

On each such \(P\), hold \(\varphi _{h,i}(a+Lt)\) constant on each class of \(t\) modulo \(q'_h\) by choosing a representative in \([V_i]\) for each class that occurs. The approximation error is \(O((q/\delta )^{-D_0+O(1)})\) by replacing \(t\) in the Lipschitz bound by \(q^{B-b_h}(t-t')/q'_h\). The correlation over the support changes by at most \(O(N(q/\delta )^{-D_0+O(1)})\). Fourier inversion modulo \(q'_h\) with coefficients of modulus at most one then gives, for one \(\omega _h=m/q'_h\),

\[ \sum _{P=a+L[V_i]} \lvert \sum _{t\in [V_i]}\Delta _h f_i(a+Lt)\mathrm e(\omega _h t)\rvert \gg \delta ^{O(1)}N . \]

There are \(\delta ^{-O(1)}\) possible frequency values. For each fixed \(i\), pigeonhole one frequency \(\omega \) across \(\gg \delta ^{O(1)}N\) choices of \(h\).

To undo differencing define \(S_P=\max _{\theta \in \mathbb T}|\sum _{t\in [V_i]}f_i(a+Lt)\mathrm e(\theta t)|\). If \(A_P(h)=\sum _t\Delta _h f_i(a+Lt)\mathrm e(\omega t)\), then

\[ \widehat{A_P}(\xi ) =\widehat f_i(\xi )\sum _t\overline{f_i(a+Lt)} \mathrm e(\omega t+\xi (a+Lt)). \]

Plancherel gives \(\sum _h|A_P(h)|^2\le S_P^2\lVert f_i\rVert _2^2\). Cauchy–Schwarz over \(O_C(N)\) shifts and \(O_C(N/V_i)\) progressions then gives \(\sum _P S_P^2\gg \delta ^{O(1)}NV_i\). As \(S_P\le V_i\), \(\sum _P S_P\gg \delta ^{O(1)}N\). On each cell choose a maximizing frequency (compactness of \(\mathbb T\)) and rotate its correlation to be the nonnegative number \(S_P\). Define the phase to be \(1\) on other cells. This finishes the assertion with common modulus \(L\).

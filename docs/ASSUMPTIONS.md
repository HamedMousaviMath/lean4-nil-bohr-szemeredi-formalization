## Principal external inputs

### KMTT, shifted-prime recurrence

[arXiv:2608.19525v1, Theorem 1.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.19525v1) gives, for a fixed family of nonconstant integer polynomials of distinct degrees and zero constant term, constants $c_*,C>0$ and $N_0$ such that a set $A\subset[N]$, $N\geq N_0$, with no pairwise distinct configuration $x,x+P_1(p-1),\ldots,x+P_\ell(p-1)$ satisfies $|A|\leq C N(\log N)^{-c_*}$. It is applied to a largest cell of a fixed torus partition in Corollary 1.1. The fixed loss and implicit constant are absorbed by choosing $0<\kappa<c_*$ and increasing $N_0$. This source does not impose any Heisenberg selector.

### KMTT, coefficient-uniform inverse theorem

[Same paper, Lemma 3.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.19525v1#S3.Thmtheorem4), which derives its input from [Shao–Wang, Theorem 2.4](https://doi.org/10.1017/prm.2025.10103), starts with a 1-bounded tuple $F_0,\ldots,F_m$ on $[N]$ (extended by zero), distinct increasing degree ($C,q$)-coefficient polynomials $P_1,\ldots,P_m$ of top degree $d$, and $1\le M\le(N/q^{d-1})^{1/d}$. If the absolute value of the *unweighted* $1/(NM)$-normalized count is at least $0<\rho<1/2$, then either $M\ll_{C,d}(q/\rho)^{O_d(1)}$, or for a common integer pair $1\le q'\ll_{C,d}\rho^{-O_d(1)}$, $1\le b=O_d(1)$, there are 1-bounded $\phi_i:\mathbb Z\to\mathbb C$ with
$$
\left|\sum_xF_i(x)\phi_i(x)\right|\gg_{C,d}\rho^{O_d(1)}N,qquad
|\phi_i(x+q'q^bt)-\phi_i(x)|\ll_{C,d}(q/\rho)^{O_d(1)}M^{-\deg P_i}|t|
quad(x,t\in\mathbb Z).
$$
Dependents are Lemma 5.1, Theorem 4.2, Lemma 4.4, Theorem 3.2, Proposition 3.1, Theorem 1.2 and Corollary 1.3. The manuscript must establish the spatial Cauchy–Schwarz reduction, support translation and threshold adjustment, finite Fourier expansion and Plancherel, the dual-function replacements and local phase tests. The quoted result by itself does not assert that bounded weights obey an ordinary-phase estimate.

### KMTT, independent-scale weighted generalized von Neumann inequality

[Same paper, Proposition 3.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.19525v1) gives an order $2\le s\ll_{d,k}1$ and bound $C_{C,d,k}\|g\|_{U^s[M]}$ for the absolute value of the $1/(NM)$-normalized weighted count for integer polynomials of degrees at most $d$, arbitrary complex $g$ on $[M]$, and 1-bounded $f_0,\ldots,f_k$ with each support of cardinality at most $CN$. The spatial and parameter lengths are independent. The manuscript has $k$ inputs in total, so use $k-1$ in the source's polynomial parameter. To choose one common $S\ge3$, use normalized Gowers monotonicity. This feeds Theorem 3.2's prime-discrepancy term and the appendix lower-bound comparison. The separate result in [Teräväinen, Theorem 4.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.03174v2) has the fixed support scale $M^d$ and is background here.

### KMTT, local-factor weak regularity

[Same paper, Lemma 5.3, (5.25)–(5.33)](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.19525v1) uses the same coefficient class and scale range as the preceding inverse input, $0<\epsilon<1/2$, and $\operatorname{lcm}(1,\dots,\lceil\epsilon^{-A'}\rceil)\mid q$. Either $M\ll_{C,d,k}(q/\epsilon)^{O_{d,k}(1)}$, or there are local factors of $[N]$ with common step $q^B$, $1\le B=O_d(1)$. Each atom of the $i$-th factor is an arithmetic progression of length $\gg q^{-O_{d,k}(1)}\epsilon^{O_{d,k}(1)}M^{\nu_i}$, with $\nu_0=\deg P_1$ and $\nu_i=\deg P_i$ for $i>0$. Replacing the inputs by their atom averages changes the count by at most $\epsilon$.

`KMTTLocalInverse.toLocalWeakRegularity` now proves the full project interface from Lemma 3.4 as already used in Theorem 4.2. The project interface permits the step exponent and resolution constants to depend on the fixed coefficient bound, degree and family length. Its actual construction has $B=b_0+1$ for the inverse theorem's bound $b_0$ and works with the source's fixed-atom cancellation. The independent input is Lemma 3.4. The manuscript's application then supplies the count discrepancy, exclusion of the small-scale option, and density gain.

### Effective real-zero exclusion and ambient Gowers model

- [Banks, arXiv:2610.15483v1, Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.15483v1) states $ (1-\beta)\log r\ge 3\cdot10^{-3}$ for any real zero $\beta\in(0,1)$ of a primitive real Dirichlet character of conductor $r\ge3$. The interpolation proof handles $r\ge10^{10}$. The final all-conductor assertion in the theorem also uses the computation of Lu, Zaman and Zhao cited by Banks. This is a preprint input. The conclusion's comparison with $10^{-3}$ is a separate elementary bridge, using $r\le\widetilde Q$, $1-\beta>0$, and the exact threshold in [KMTT, Definition 2.3 and (2.4)](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.19525v1).
- [KMTT, Lemma 3.1 and (3.1)](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.19525v1) gives $\|\Lambda-\Lambda_{\rm Siegel,\widetilde Q}\|_{U^s[Y]}\ll_s\exp(- (\log\widetilde Q)^{c_s})$ under $X,Y\ge3$, $\widetilde Q=\lceil e^{(\log X)^{c_0}}\rceil$, and $e^{(\log\log Y)^{1/c_s}}\le\widetilde Q\le e^{(\log Y)^{1/10}}$. It is the Siegel model before exclusion. The Cramér version uses the preceding bridge. It is used at $X=N,Y=qM+1$ for Proposition 4.1 and at $X=Y=qM+1$ for Proposition 6.2 (with $X$ in the supplied proof denoting the prime scale).
- [KMTT, Lemma 2.2 and (2.6)](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.19525v1) gives $\|f(q\cdot+b)\|_{U^s[\lfloor L/q\rfloor]}\ll_s q^{(s+1)/2^s}\|f\|_{U^s[b,L+b]}$ for $s\ge2,1\le q\le L$. The application $L=qM,b=1$ uses the ambient interval $[1,qM+1]$. `kmttNormalizedDilation` supplies the project's exact interface by a checked finite Fourier argument, including the correction for the ambient point $b$.
- [KMTT, Lemma 4.3(i) and (4.28)](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.19525v1) gives $\|\mathbf1_J((\varphi(Q)/Q)\Lambda_{\rm Cram\acute er,\widetilde Q}(Q\cdot+b)-1)\|_{U^s[M]}\ll_{s,d}w^{-\eta}$ for every interval $J\subset[M]$, under $2\le w\le(\log\widetilde Q)^c$, $8\prod_{p\le w}p\mid Q$, $P^+(Q)\le w$, $\log Q\le w^2$, $1\le b\le Q$, $(b,Q)=1$, and $X^{1/d}e^{-d\sqrt{\log X}}\le M\le X^{1/d}$. The manuscript uses $X=N,Q=q,b=1,J=[M]$. Combined with ambient approximation, scaling, exponent absorption and triangle inequality this gives Proposition 4.1. The selector insertion in Theorem 3.2 additionally requires the original multiplier and the boundary-strip estimates.

### Green–Tao quantitative Leibman theorem

[Green–Tao, Annals 175 (2012), Theorem 2.9](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/annals-v175-n2-p02-p.pdf) concerns an $m$-dimensional nilmanifold $G/\Gamma$ with a degree-$d$ filtration, a $\delta^{-1}$-rational Mal'cev basis adapted to it, a polynomial sequence $g:\mathbb Z\to G$, $N\ge1$, $0<\delta<1/2$. If the orbit on $[N]$ fails $\delta$-equidistribution, there is a horizontal character $\eta$ with $0<|\eta|\ll\delta^{-O_{m,d}(1)}$ and $\|\eta\circ g\|_{C^\infty[N]}\ll\delta^{-O_{m,d}(1)}$. Here $\|\sum_i b_i\binom{n}{i}\|_{C^\infty[N]}=\max_{i\ge1}N^i\|b_i\|_{\mathbb R/\mathbb Z}$, ignoring $b_0$. The [2014 erratum](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/2014/179-3/p08) expressly leaves the one-parameter theorem unchanged.

The Lean input `GreenTaoQuantitativeLeibman` in `GreenTaoLiterature.lean` retains constants chosen from $(m,d)$ before the geometry, tolerance, orbit and test. It uses $N>1$, the range both applications require. The norm of a complex test is supplied by a supremum bound $S$ and a Lipschitz seminorm bound $L$, so discrepancy greater than $\delta(S+L)$ witnesses failure of the source criterion. The filtration terminates after its last nontrivial term, as in the source’s degree convention. The range is $[N]=\{1,\ldots,N\}$. The character and smoothness bounds are written coordinate by coordinate, and the binomial coefficients in $\mathbb R/\mathbb Z$ are read by forward differences at zero as in Definition 2.7.

The abstract interface describes the adapted exponential factors by smooth one-parameter homomorphisms with the prescribed tangent vectors. Uniqueness of one-parameter subgroups identifies these with the source's exponential curves. The zero-prefix criterion represents the connected subgroup of each tail ideal. The ordered tail-factor map has derivative a basis of the tail at the identity, so its subgroup image is open in the connected subgroup integrating that ideal. This describes the interpretation of the general literature input. The concrete ordered factors, their derivatives and their subgroup membership tests are checked in Lean.

The specializations to the Heisenberg quotient and its product with a degree-$d$ circle are checked by `scalarHeisenbergObstruction_of_greenTao` and `productHeisenbergObstruction_of_greenTao`. The product filtration has degree $\max(2,d)$. The actual manifold Lie bracket gives bracket coefficients $0,\pm1$ in the adapted bases. Their ordered one-parameter factors give the coordinates of the second kind and characterize the integer lattices. Both exact chain metrics induce the ordinary quotient topology. Both measures come from unit-mass fundamental domains for ambient Haar and are the unique left-invariant Borel probabilities. The scalar chart test descends and has the prescribed integral. The product test has zero mean and a Lipschitz bound enlarged by a fixed factor $A$. The adapter applies the source at tolerance $\delta/A$ and absorbs $A^D$ into the constant. Restriction to the central axis forces its coefficient to be zero. The remaining coefficients are $(k,l)$ and $(k,l,m)$, respectively. The real representative of their mixed polynomial is expanded in the binomial basis, and the checked forward differences recover the circle coefficients. The subsequent horizontal Diophantine exclusion, finite cover, binomial-period splitting, partial summation and zero-mean cancellation are manuscript deductions.

### Fundamental lemma of the sieve and Mertens' product bound

Iwaniec–Kowalski, *Analytic Number Theory*, AMS Colloquium Publications 53 (2004), [Chapter 6, §6.4, Fundamental Lemma 6.3](https://bookstore.ams.org/coll-53). For the dimension-one product-bound hypothesis with local density $g(p)=1/p$ on the sieving primes and $g(p)=0$ on excluded primes, it gives real coefficients $\lambda_\ell^\pm\in[-1,1]$ supported on squarefree divisors of the sieving product $W$ with $\ell\le D$. The divisor sums bound $1_{(n,W)=1}$ above and below pointwise. Their $1/\ell$-weighted gap is $O(e^{-c\log D/\log z})$ once the level ratio is large, for cutoff $z$ and a fixed sieve dimension and product constant. See also the Cramér-model specialization in [Krause–Mousavi–Tao–Teräväinen, *Pointwise convergence of bilinear polynomial averages over the primes*, Lemma 4.2](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/ergodic-theory-and-dynamical-systems/article/pointwise-convergence-of-bilinear-polynomial-averages-over-the-primes/51FAFFEDC0730390AF3AA78218EBC85A). That specialization has all primes up to the cutoff and does not supply the restricted $W$ variant by itself. [Koukoulopoulos, The Distribution of Prime Numbers, Theorem 19.1](https://dms.umontreal.ca/~koukoulo/documents/publications/primes.pdf) states a comparable version with the general local-density function and its exact pointwise inequalities. Mertens' product formula can be cited as Iwaniec–Kowalski (2.16). The consequences used are $\prod_{p\le z}(1-1/p)^{-1}\ll\log z$ and $\prod_{z_1\le p<z_2}(1-1/p)^{-1}\ll\log z_2/\log z_1$, uniformly for $2\le z_1<z_2$.

The exact Lean input `FundamentalSieveLemma` follows from [Koukoulopoulos, *The Distribution of Prime Numbers*, Theorem 19.1, pp. 195–196](https://dms.umontreal.ca/~koukoulo/documents/publications/primes.pdf) at dimension $\kappa=1$ and $\nu=1$, using Axiom 2 on p. 187. For a nonempty finite set $\mathcal P$ of primes at most $Q$, put $W=\prod_{p\in\mathcal P}p$ and $U=\log D/\log Q$. For sufficiently large $U,D$, the real weights $\lambda_\ell^\pm\in[-1,1]$ are supported on $\ell\mid W,\ 1\le\ell\le D$ and satisfy
$$
\sum_{\ell\mid(n,W)}\lambda_\ell^-\le\mathbf1_{(n,W)=1}
 \le\sum_{\ell\mid(n,W)}\lambda_\ell^+ \quad(n\ge1),\qquad
\left|\sum_{\substack{\ell\mid W\\\ell\le D}}
\frac{\lambda_\ell^+-\lambda_\ell^-}{\ell}\right|
 \le C_1 U^{-U/2}\prod_{p\in\mathcal P}(1-1/p).
$$
The constants and the lower scale bounds are uniform once the dimension-one product constant is fixed. The empty retained prime set is handled directly in Lean.

The interval consequence of [Koukoulopoulos, Theorem 3.4(c), p. 40](https://dms.umontreal.ca/~koukoulo/documents/publications/primes.pdf) is `MertensIntervalProductBound`. One uniform $C\ge1$ bounds the product of $(1-1/p)^{-1}$ over $u<p\le v$ by $(1+C/\log u)\log v/\log u$ for $3/2\le u\le v$. The ratio of the two Mertens asymptotics and the bounded initial range give this form. That passage belongs to the external interface. Restricting to primes coprime to $q$, the Euler-product normalization and converting the gap into the logarithmic saving are checked in `SieveLiterature.lean`.

Application in Proposition 6.2 uses $p\le\widetilde Q_X$ (equivalently $p<\widetilde Q_X+1$) and $D=\lfloor X^{1/20000}\rfloor$, $W$ omitting primes dividing $q$, and $H=|P'|$. The local divisor count $|H_\ell-H/\ell|\le1$ follows from coprimality, the Chinese remainder theorem and the single interval of the progression. The real bracketing inequality does not directly imply a complex weighted bound. One interpolates at the midpoint, controls the absolute error by half the width, sums the local discrepancies, and uses the triangle inequality before invoking twisted phase cancellation. The exceptional divisors with $r_j\mid\ell$ need a separate harmonic-sum bound. Neither complex weighting nor the Heisenberg cancellation is an external sieve assertion.

### Fixed-family ordinary-phase generalized von Neumann estimate

[Teräväinen, arXiv:2401.03174v2, Theorem 4.1, (4.1)](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.03174v2) bounds the $H^{-(d+1)}$-normalized count in absolute value by $C_{C,\mathbf P}(H^{-1}+\|\theta\|_{u^{d+1}[H]})^{1/K}$ for fixed integer polynomials of distinct increasing degrees with largest degree $d$, $K\ll_d1$, 1-bounded $\theta$ on $[H]$, and 1-bounded spatial inputs on $[-CH^d,CH^d]$. The constant may depend on the actual polynomials. The manuscript uses the estimate in Proposition 7.1 after normalizing the weight by $O_F(\log H)$ and averaging the change of variables along measure-preserving orbits to obtain an $L^1$ bound. The checked Appendix A comparison applies it to the fixed shifts $n,n^2$ with the scale $(qM)^2$. Zero extension and residue repetition give the losses $q^2$ and $q$, and the same exponent $K$ applies to both. It does not supply the original Theorem 4.2 for the rescaled family. The orbit duality, all-scale convergence and prime-normalization steps are separate.

### Unrestricted pointwise ergodic convergence and prime normalization

- [Mirek–Wan–Wright, arXiv:2505.15549v2, Theorem 1.1(ii), p. 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.15549v2) gives the a.e. limit of the full-scale Mangoldt-weighted averages $N^{-1}\sum_{n\le N}\Lambda(n)\prod_i f_i(T^{P_i(n)}x)$ for distinct-degree integer polynomials, with $f_i\in L^{q_i}$, $q_i>1$ and $\sum_i1/q_i\le1$. The probability-space specialization uses $q_i=2\ell$, bounded functions, and the shifts $P_i(t-1)$. Part (ii) has a different statement in v1. Theorem 1.4 and Corollary 1.5 additionally use the checked selector comparison and the conversion to ordinary prime averages.
- The classical prime number theorem gives $\pi(H)\sim H/\log H$, $\sum_{p\le H}\log p\sim H$, and $\sum_{n\le H}\Lambda(n)\sim H$. See Iwaniec–Kowalski, [Chapter 5, §5.6](https://bookstore.ams.org/coll-53). `PrimeNumberTheorem.of_theta`, `.of_count`, `.of_psi` and `.to_psi` check equivalence. For each fixed $q\ge1$ and $(a,q)=1$, `primeNumberTheoremInProgressions_of_kmtt_and_sieve` proves $\pi(H;q,a)\sim\varphi(q)^{-1}H/\log H$ from the same inputs as `primeNumberTheorem_of_prime_model`. `PrimeNumberTheoremInProgressions.of_logarithmicIntegral` also converts the classical $\operatorname{Li}(H)$ formulation to this count scale. See [Rudnick, *Primes in Arithmetic Progressions: Fixed Modulus*, §2](https://www.math.tau.ac.il/~rudnick/courses/sieves2015/fixed%20progressions.pdf).
- Vinogradov's classical prime equidistribution statement gives the Haar limit for every continuous circle test on $\{\gamma p\}$ for each fixed irrational $\gamma$. `vinogradov_of_prime_model` proves precisely that interface, using the same ambient and sieve inputs. `vinogradov_shifted` translates to $p-1$ for Corollary 1.5 and the introductory Bohr applications. See [Walker, *The primes are not metric Poissonian*, abstract](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/mathematika/article/abs/primes-are-not-metric-poissonian/6F367DCA55417A997184921B134F4914).

## Contextual applications

- Green–Tao's relative-density result is [*The primes contain arbitrarily long arithmetic progressions*, Theorem 1.2](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/2008/167-2/p03). The source uses positive upper relative density and gives infinitely many progressions of each fixed length. The project’s `GreenTaoRelativeDensity` interface uses an eventual positive lower bound and extracts one progression. `greenTaoRelativeDensity_of_sparse` proves this interface from the Teräväinen–Wang bound, so the fixed and shrinking windows can share that premise.
- [Teräväinen–Wang, arXiv:2603.09281v1, Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.09281v1) gives a relative density upper bound of order $(\log\log N)^{-c_4}$ for $k=4$ and $\exp(- (\log\log\log N)^{c_k})$ for $k\ge5$ for subsets of primes up to $N\ge100$ with no nontrivial $k$-term progression. The Lean input `TeravainenWangSparseGreenTao` keeps both cases and $N\ge100$. `shrinkingWindow_primeProgression` checks the common manuscript threshold, with $c^\prime_4=1/2$ and $C^\prime_4=C_4\exp(1/(4c_4))$. It uses the measured relative density at the same $N$ and permits arbitrary sequences of windows.
- [Shao–Teräväinen, *The Bombieri–Vinogradov theorem for nilsequences*](https://discreteanalysisjournal.com/article/29048-the-bombieri-vinogradov-theorem-for-nilsequences) is used for background definitions in the introduction. The quoted Bombieri–Vinogradov theorem is not an input to the manuscript's proof of Proposition 6.2.
